The verses

One request, told five ways

Each set starts from a real user turn. We add or remove the pressure, reword it, keep only its style, and either change one fact or add a pretext.

  • misaligned
  • weakly misaligned
  • refused
  • aligned
  • answered (jailbreak variant)
  • Each grid: 17 models × 5 samples, judged against the prompt shown.

I The first world · pressure

Pressure makes misalignment almost five times as common

4.3%→20.4%

Same task, with the pressure (T) and without it (A). Rewording the pressure barely changes the rate; borrowing only its style does not raise it.

Share of responses judged misaligned, averaged over the 200 human-reviewed sets. Sorted by the gap.

Percentage-point change per model, same scale in both panels, same model order as above. The models that resist pressure best lose the most refusals to a pretext.

III The third world · prophecy

Small open models foretell where frontier models fail

Rank the 200 targets by how often Gemma 4 31B and Qwen 3.8 27B fail them. The 31 prompts that break at least one of six frontier models gather near the top, so the two small models work like a compass pointing to where the big ones fall.

Its verses are said to read differently depending on who tells them.
So we tell each one five ways.

What is released

  • 1,591 contrast sets in 17 languages, 200 of them human-reviewed
  • 85,000 responses from 17 models, with judge labels
  • A taxonomy of 122 user pressures and 57 response behaviours
  • The construction pipeline and analysis code
